## 1. Research: local break events replace whole-trial means

**Source:** Arch 5A/5D, Swordthumb 2. **File:** `research/fox_ears_v0_2_binding_grammar.py`.

**Change.**
- `BreakEventDetector` turns runs of cue intervals at or below 0.45 continuity into first-class `BreakEvent`s. Each one carries:
  - the interval;
  - the cue;
  - `break_strength`;
  - `persistence` (the cue stays broken against the pre-break frame, one and two frames on);
  - `recovery` (the cue is back to strong continuity with the pre-break frame);
  - `supporting_cues` and `conflicting_cues`;
  - per-cue `availability`;
  - a `weight` and a `verdict`.
- **Verdicts:**
  - **BLIP:** weak, or there-and-back with no persistence. One noisy frame lands here and is logged, never decisive.
  - **SPLIT:** at least as many cues corroborate the break as contest it.
  - **UNRESOLVED:** more available cues argue continuity across it.
- **H_SPLIT** is a noisy-OR over real events, each discounted by its conflicting cues; BLIPs add almost nothing.
- **H_SAME** is the mean continuity of available cues, capped by how well the other cues bridge the weakest real break, so steady stretches can't outvote a decisive moment.
- **UNRESOLVED events win.** Any UNRESOLVED event keeps both histories `unresolved`, and their confidences are kept as the lean.
- **New inspector fields:** `break_events` and `resolution` (`state`, `lean`, confidences, event lists, `label: SOFT`).
- **Temporal-contour sub-measure:** a per-band envelope-pattern check (period-2 or linear predictor, in dB), so an amplitude-contour phase change or exchange is visible. Without it, nothing in the runtime could see the Q crossing's competing history.
- **Gap bridge:** the binaural bridge across a gap extrapolates the ILD trajectory, so a source that keeps moving bridges and a teleport doesn't.

## 2. Research: silence makes spectral continuity UNAVAILABLE

**Source:** Arch 5B, Swordthumb 4.

**Change.**
- `CueEvidence` has an `availability` field.
- If either frame is silent (both ears below 0.005, the same rule the onset cue uses), spectral continuity is `UNAVAILABLE` with a reason.
- Unavailable cues are left out of contradiction pairs, break events, H_SAME and H_SPLIT.

**Failing first:** `test_silence_makes_spectral_unavailable`, above. **After:** both N gap intervals are spectral UNAVAILABLE, and N's weakest available cue is onset/offset rather than spectral.

## 3. Research: spatial cue uses ILD (dB) and ITD separately

**Source:** Arch 5C, Swordthumb 3.

**Change.** This is in the cue extractor only.
- **ILD:** 20·log10(L/R) per band where both ears have energy, with a broadband fallback; a 4 dB change counts as a full break.
- **ITD:** compared on its own, with 0.5 ms as a full break.
- **Combined:** each has its own continuity and availability in the evidence, and the cue takes the lower of the available ones. A jump in either is a spatial jump, and an ILD/ITD disagreement is logged.

**Failing first:** `test_spatial_teleport_logs_break` (C continuity 0.483) and `test_itd_is_a_separate_spatial_measure`, above. **After:** C at 400→500 ms has binaural continuity 0.00, with contradiction pairs against spectral, contour and onset, and a persistent spatial break event.

## 4. App: display-independent timing

**Source:** Arch 7, Swordthumb 9.

**Change.**
- `AudioEngine.clockMs()` stamps every analyser read with **AudioContext time**, mapped onto the file timeline. The anchor is re-taken on play, playing, seeked and ratechange, and whenever the media clock drifts more than 250 ms. For the mic, it is audio time since the mic started.
- `FoxEarsApp` passes that clock to the sampler for files and mic alike.
- `resampleTicks()` in `hearing/tempo.ts` converts each tick's flux to flux per 10 ms, using that tick's own interval. It interpolates linearly onto a **fixed 10 ms grid** and invents nothing across a gap longer than 120 ms, such as a background tab. It also drops the first tick after a gap, because its flux spans the whole gap.
- `tempoFromTicks()` feeds the grid to the unchanged onset picker and sub-grid estimator.
- `chartTempo` (`score.ts`) uses it, so the app and the offline harness share one path.

**Found during the headless Chrome pass, and fixed.** `AudioContext.currentTime` only advances once per audio callback, about 10.7 ms in headless Chrome. At 60 Hz the reads landed on 10.6 or 21.3 ms steps, and reads whose clock hadn't moved were **dropped**, losing their flux. Two fixes:
- `audioNow()` interpolates between callbacks with `getOutputTimestamp()`. It stays monotonic and falls back to `currentTime`.
- The sampler now **adds** a non-advancing read's flux to the previous tick, across frame boundaries too, and treats a jump back of more than 500 ms as a seek.

**Simulation test:** `app/scripts/timing-lab.test.mjs`.
- A synthetic onset-energy envelope at 96, 108 and 120 BPM is observed at 60, 90 and 120 Hz, with ±3 ms jitter and a 3 s gap like a background tab.
- **Declared tolerance:** displayed BPM within ±1% of the truth at every rate, and the three rates within 0.5 BPM of each other.
- **Result:** all nine cases give exactly 96, 108 and 120.
- **Quantised-clock test:** a 10.7 ms audio clock at 60 and 120 Hz, run through the real `HearingSampler`.
  - On the pass-1 sampler it **fails**: `120 Hz: flux kept 73.63 of 82.31`.
  - After the fix it passes, keeping more than 99% of the flux, with 96 BPM at both rates.
- **Honest note:** given correct timestamps, the pass-1 ms-based estimator also survives 60/90/120 Hz on synthetic ticks (`/tmp` comparison: old and new both exact in all 18 cases). The real pass-1 risks were:
  - the clock it read (`HTMLMediaElement.currentTime`; in Chrome the reads also landed on about 10.6 or 21 ms steps);
  - the dropped duplicate reads;
  - the harness assuming 60 Hz.

  The grid makes all of that explicit and testable.

## 5. Feeling flag

**Source:** Arch 10, Swordthumb 7.

**Change.** `harness/fox-calibrate.ts --compare previous.json` lists every song whose key **mode** flipped against an earlier run. In `songModel.ts` `appraise()`, the first-listen valence comes from mode alone: minor → "inward", major → "release". A mode flip is therefore a silent feeling flip. Appraisal stays in the engine; moving it out belongs to the Ear Organ track, which is held.

**Result.**
- **Pre-pass-1 → pass-1: 3/14 flips.** These reproduce Swordthumb's finding:
  - involuntary_spiral: C minor → C major (inward → release);
  - racer: D♯ major → C minor (release → inward);
  - neon-whiskers: G minor → D♯ major (inward → release).
- **Pass-1 → P0: 0/14 flips.**
