## Ground rules kept

- **Additive only.** The new layer reads what the chart already decided (10 ms grid frames, beat grid, sections, key) and writes one new field, `chart.sonar`. It never feeds back into key, tempo, chords or sections. This was checked byte for byte; see "Additivity" below.
- **Measure once, hear many times.** No model weights went into the browser app. Beat This!, All-In-One and the vocal separator are research-side candidates (`harness/candidates.py`), scored beside Fox Ears and never merged into it.
- **Licences are labelled.** SongFormer is deliberately not wrapped: its MuQ weights are CC BY-NC 4.0. Every new registry entry carries a `license_class`.
- **No tuning to a fixture.** The thresholds below were set on synthetic chord frames and the etude only. They were not tuned on the 14-song set, whose audio is not in this environment.

## What "sonar" means here

Active sonar needs a known ping; a finished recording has none, so Fox Ears cannot ping a song. What does transfer is sonar's maths: compare the signal with delayed copies of itself. Every bar is held up against every other bar, and the bars that come back are the song's echoes. The matched filter (the riff finder) is the one literal transfer: slide a known "ping" (a riff) along the song and listen for its returns.

Blind room or reverb estimation from a mixed song was **not** attempted. The literature cited in the brief finds music unsuitable for it, because reverb is a production choice, not a room.

## What landed

| Piece | File | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Echo map + letters | `app/src/lib/hearing/sonar.ts` | Beat-synchronous chroma + bass chroma. Each bar's fingerprint is its four beat vectors end to end (a one-bar time-delay embedding), giving a bar × bar self-similarity matrix. It also yields repetition letters per 4-bar phrase (A A B A', with "–" for silence), the **loop** (shortest cycle that repeats straight away), the **form period** (the repeat covering the most bars), a downbeat-phase estimate (4/4 assumed), and **agreement with FORM**: which section cuts sonar also hears, which it hears that FORM missed, and which FORM made where the harmony repeats. |
| Riff finder | `app/src/lib/hearing/motif.ts` | Matched filter on bass-led beat chroma, in all 12 transpositions, with non-max suppression. By default the template is the first instance of the most-repeated phrase. Any bar range works (`findMotifAt`). Reports each return's bar, score and transposition (+5 = moved up to the IV). |
| Tension ribbon | `app/src/lib/hearing/spiral.ts` | A native reimplementation of Chew's spiral array with Herremans & Chew's three measures, per bar: cloud diameter (spread), cloud momentum (motion) and tensile strain (distance from the measured key). Chroma-based, so there is no octave or voicing. |
| Bar brief | `app/src/lib/hearing/barBrief.ts` | One text line per bar for an AI listener: `b17 0:42.1 \| sec B·chorus \| son A(2) \| I V vi IV \| bass 1→5 \| mel 3 2 1 \| str .42↑ \| vox \| riff+5 \| "words"`. It also writes the digest's SONAR, TENSION RIBBON and BAR BRIEF blocks, including a phrase × phrase similarity table labelled with letters. Text, not images: exact tokens beat a model's reading of a spectrogram. |

## Etude scores (SOFT: planted synthetic truth, not Taylor's music)

`harness/score_answer_key.py`, mir_eval 0.8.2:

| | Master, 100 BPM | 108% stretch |
|---|---|---|
| Tempo | 100 vs 100, STRICT | 108 vs 108, STRICT |
| Key | G major, weighted 1.00 | G major, weighted 1.00 |
| Beats F (±70 ms) | **0.000** (see below) | 0.995 |
| Downbeats F (sonar bars) | **0.000** | 1.000 |
| FORM sections, ±3 s F | 0.50 | 0.38 |
| **SONAR** sections, ±3 s F | **0.88** | **0.88** |
| FORM pairwise labels F | 0.49 | 0.51 |
| **SONAR** pairwise labels F | **0.98** | **1.00** |
| Chords root | 0.674 | 0.638 |

The etude's repetition reads `A A A B B' A A B B' C B B' A`, which is the planted form at 4-bar resolution: the chorus's two halves are B and B'. On the master, FORM got four of the seven true cuts. It missed three (0:29.1, 0:48.3, 1:07.5) and added three (0:43.5, 0:53.1, 1:17.1). Sonar's agreement check flags all six. It also flags 0:09.9, a true cut that FORM got right but sonar cannot hear, because the intro and verse share the same chords. The riff finder returns the verse riff at bars 1, 5, 9, 21, 25 and 49, and at **bar 37, transposed +5** (the bridge).

**Do not read this as "sonar beats FORM".** The etude is built from the same instruments throughout, with sections that differ only in harmony. That is sonar's best case and FORM's worst, because FORM also weighs energy and vocal entry. Sonar cannot hear a change that keeps the chords (the intro→verse cut at 0:09.9), and it treats a transposed section as new (the bridge is C; only the riff finder sees +5). The point is that the two methods fail differently, which is what a second opinion is for.

**Observation on the existing beat grid (SOFT, not changed here).** On the master, Fox Ears' grid sat about 0.26 s early, roughly half a beat. Tempo was exact, but beat and downbeat F were 0. On the 108% stretch the phase was right (0.995). The etude has equal-level hi-hats on every 8th, which is a plausible trap for a phase picker. P4 does not touch `tempo.ts`, which is guarded by the 14-song set. This is the case Beat This! downbeats should be scored against, now that the scorer and candidate adapter exist.
